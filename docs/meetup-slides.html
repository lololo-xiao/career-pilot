Open by naming the tension: job search is repetitive enough to automate, but personal enough that unsupported claims can do real harm. CareerPilot is my answer to that tension.

The problem

Faster is not automatically better

Generic AI makes candidates faster at producing words. It does not make those words defensible.

01

Context resets

The candidate keeps re-explaining goals, experience, and constraints.

02

Evidence disappears

Advice sounds confident without showing which claim supports it.

03

Work is fragmented

Discovery, tailoring, tracking, and preparation live in separate tools.

04

Automation gets risky

A polished false claim or unreviewed submission can damage trust.

The product is not “ChatGPT with a job-search prompt.” The problem is continuity, grounding, and control across a long-running workflow.

The product thesis

One agent, one evidence trail, one controlled loop

The job search becomes a loop Pilot can carry with you.

Actual product · grounded fit

Feedback with citations and an honesty boundary

Every fit opinion has to survive an evidence check.

DemonstratedDirect candidate evidence supports the requirement.
AdjacentTransferable experience exists; the exact claim does not.
MissingThe gap remains visible and becomes a preparation action.

The agent can say “you are close” without rewriting “close” into “experienced.”

Use the Kubernetes example: Docker and CI/CD evidence does not prove production Kubernetes ownership. This is the product’s differentiator in one sentence.

Truth boundaryNo unsupported claim becomes a match or tailored fact.
Action boundaryPreview and approve sensitive operations; tokens are scoped and expiring.
Evolution boundaryVersion, evaluate, quarantine, activate, or roll back user-owned revisions.
This slide answers “why local?” and “what can the agent do?” Avoid claiming public multi-user readiness; current deployment is deliberately device-local.
